The image shows medieval marginalia from the Smithfield Decretals (created around the 1340s) depicting anthropomorphic rabbits turning the tables to capture, try, and execute a human hunter. [1, 2]
![](https://i.pinimg.com/736x/0e/44/79/0e447910cb6b8121533921fc9af6f63e.jpg)
## Key Details of the Artwork
- Smithfield Decretals: This famous 14th-century manuscript is housed in the [British Library](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/m/01bmj) and contains decorated legal texts. [1, 2, 3]
- Marginal Drolleries: Medieval artists frequently drew bizarre, humorous, or upside-down scenes in the margins (drolleries) where prey animals like hares and rabbits act as fierce warriors. [3, 4]
- The Narrative Sequence: The panels show a rabbit shooting the hunter with a bow, the rabbits tying him up for trial before a rabbit judge, and finally executing him. [2]

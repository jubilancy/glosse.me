The collection is renowned for its visual heritage, particularly in the realms of alchemy, natural history, and the "theatres of machines." Works by authors like Heinrich Khunrath, Michael Maier, and Robert Fludd are celebrated for their intricate, symbolic engravings.

### Alchemical and Esoteric Emblems
The collection is particularly strong in alchemical emblem books, where the image is not merely a decoration but the primary vehicle for transmitting philosophical and chemical secrets.

*   **Heinrich Khunrath's *Amphitheatrum Sapientiae Aeternae***: Perhaps the most famous alchemical work in the collection, featuring large-scale, symbolic engravings that integrate laboratory practice with Christian Kabbalah. The "Alchemist's Laboratory" plate is a masterpiece of perspective and symbolism.
*   **Hieronymus Reusner's *[Pandora](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/pandora-das-ist-die-edlest-gab-gottes-oder-der-werde-und-reusner-3)*** (1588): A richly illustrated German work that presents the Philosopher's Stone through a series of complex allegories.
*   **Jacob Bosch's *[Symbolographia](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/symbolographia-sive-de-arte-symbolica-sermones-septem-bosch-2)*** (1702): This monumental work contains over 2,000 small emblem illustrations across nearly 800 pages, categorizing symbols into sacred, heroic, ethical, and satirical classes.

### The Theatres of Machines and Early Technology
The library holds 78 books in the **Theatres of Machines** collection, which document the mechanical marvels of the Renaissance and Enlightenment.

*   **Georg Agricola's *[De re metallica](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/georgii-agricolae-de-re-metallica-libri-xii-quibus-officia-agricola-2)*** (1556): Famous for its hundreds of detailed woodcuts showing mining techniques, smelting furnaces, and mechanical pumps. It remained the standard text on mining for over two centuries.
*   **Roberto Valturio's *[On Military Matters](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/de-re-militari-libris-xii-valturio)*** (1483): One of the earliest printed books with technical illustrations, featuring ingenious (and sometimes fantastical) siege engines and military hardware.

### Making Things Visible: The Birth of Microscopy
There are 26 books in the **Making Things Visible** collection, which includes the first works to reveal the "microworld" through high-quality engravings.

*   **Robert Hooke's *[Micrographia](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/micrographia-or-some-physiological-descriptions-of-minute-hooke)*** (1665): Renowned for its large, folding copperplate engravings of insects and plants as seen through a microscope, including the famous image of a flea.
*   **Antoni van Leeuwenhoek's *[Anatomies and Discoveries](https://sourcelibrary.org/book/ontledingen-en-ontdekkingen-leeuwenhoek)*** (1685): Contains the first visual records of bacteria, sperm, and other microscopic structures.
